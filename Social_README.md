# Social Stratification Analysis

## Aim
This project investigates whether mental health pressures are experienced equally across different demographic groups in England.
Using population, life satisfaction, mental health service use, and Mental Health Act detention data, the analysis explores how experiences differ by race, age, and socioeconomic deprivation.
The aim is not only to identify which groups experience higher levels of mental health pressure, but also to examine how wellbeing, engagement with support services, and crisis-level interventions vary across the population.

## Overview
Mental health outcomes are not experienced equally across society. Factors such as age, ethnicity, and socioeconomic deprivation can influence wellbeing, access to support, and the likelihood of reaching crisis point.

This project explores three key dimensions of social stratification:

* Race
* Age
* Deprivation

For each dimension, four measures are examined:

1. Population – providing a baseline for comparison.
2. Life Satisfaction – used as an indicator of overall wellbeing and potential life friction.
3. Mental Health Service Use – representing engagement with mental health support services.
4. Mental Health Act Detentions – used as a marker of severe mental health crisis requiring compulsory intervention.

By comparing these measures over time, the analysis seeks to identify patterns of inequality and explore where differences emerge between groups.

Rather than viewing mental health solely through the lens of illness, this project considers how broader social circumstances may influence wellbeing, support-seeking behaviour, and crisis outcomes. The findings aim to contribute to a wider discussion on mental health inequalities and the role that social factors may play in shaping mental health experiences.

## Data obtained:
Although it was not possible to obtain all of the data required to answer the original research question, analyses were completed where sufficient and comparable data were available. Population-adjusted measures and visualisations were created to explore meaningful patterns within the available evidence.

Rather than forcing conclusions from incomplete data, only analyses supported by robust and comparable datasets were included. The remaining elements of the framework have been retained as areas for future investigation should suitable data become available.

## Further Research & Limitations : Social Stratification and Mental Health Inequalities

A proposed section of this project aimed to explore whether mental health pressures are experienced equally across different demographic groups in England.

The objective was not only to identify which groups experience higher levels of mental health pressure, but also to examine how wellbeing, engagement with support services, and crisis-level interventions vary across the population. The analysis sought to explore how broader social circumstances may influence mental health experiences, support-seeking behaviour, and crisis outcomes.

Three demographic dimensions were selected for investigation:

* Race
* Age
* Socioeconomic deprivation

These dimensions were chosen because the ways in which people are organised within society can create both barriers and sources of support. Factors such as cultural attitudes, social expectations, economic circumstances, and access to resources may influence how mental health difficulties are experienced and managed.

Initial exploration produced several findings of interest. Population-adjusted detention rates suggested notable differences between ethnic groups, with some groups appearing disproportionately represented in Mental Health Act detentions. Similarly, unexpectedly high detention rates were observed among adults aged 65 and over, raising questions about the potential influence of factors such as physical health, social isolation, bereavement, and loss of independence.

The proposed analytical framework aimed to examine four stages:

Population → Wellbeing → Service Use → Crisis Intervention

The intention was to investigate whether different groups experience different forms of friction before reaching crisis point, and whether these patterns are reflected in help-seeking behaviour and detention outcomes.

However, this analysis was ultimately paused due to data limitations. While population and detention data were available, equivalent measures of wellbeing and mental health service use were not consistently available across all demographic groups and reporting periods. In particular, ethnicity-based service use data could not be obtained in a format comparable to the other variables, and wellbeing measures were not consistently available beyond the 2022–23 reporting period.

As a result, it was not possible to construct a sufficiently robust and comparable framework across race, age, and deprivation. Rather than drawing conclusions from incomplete evidence, the analysis was deferred for future research.

Future investigation could explore how cultural attitudes towards mental health, help-seeking behaviours, social labelling, deprivation, and other forms of social friction influence engagement with mental health services and the likelihood of crisis intervention. Access to more comprehensive NHS, ONS, or linked administrative datasets would allow a more complete examination of mental health inequalities across demographic groups.
