# Social Stratification Analysis

## Aim
This project investigates whether mental health pressures are experienced equally across different demographic groups in England.
Using population, life satisfaction, mental health service use, and Mental Health Act detention data, the analysis explores how experiences differ by race, age, and socioeconomic deprivation.
The aim is not only to identify which groups experience higher levels of mental health pressure, but also to examine how wellbeing, engagement with support services, and crisis-level interventions vary across the population.

## Overview
Mental health outcomes are not experienced equally across society. Factors such as age, ethnicity, and socioeconomic deprivation can influence wellbeing, access to support, and the likelihood of reaching crisis point.

This project explores three key dimensions of social stratification:

* Race
* Age
* Deprivation

For each dimension, four measures are examined:

1. Population – providing a baseline for comparison.
2. Life Satisfaction – used as an indicator of overall wellbeing and potential life friction.
3. Mental Health Service Use – representing engagement with mental health support services.
4. Mental Health Act Detentions – used as a marker of severe mental health crisis requiring compulsory intervention.

By comparing these measures over time, the analysis seeks to identify patterns of inequality and explore where differences emerge between groups.

Rather than viewing mental health solely through the lens of illness, this project considers how broader social circumstances may influence wellbeing, support-seeking behaviour, and crisis outcomes. The findings aim to contribute to a wider discussion on mental health inequalities and the role that social factors may play in shaping mental health experiences.
